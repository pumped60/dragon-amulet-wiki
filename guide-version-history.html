Version history

Every update, newest first. Each update adds one to the last number of the version (0.9.1, 0.9.2 …).

Beta 0.9.104

  • The magma realm's rock: four new blocks join magma rock and obsidian: basalt (dark grey, in bands and hanging from the ceilings of tall caverns), ember stone (black with glowing cracks; it gives a little light), scoria (porous red-black, crumbly) and magma dirt (ashy soil on the open floors, where the magma plants now grow). Obsidian comes in bigger pockets. Red magma rock is only part of the realm now. Old worlds get the new rock where you have not been.
  • Bigger realm castles: the Sky Ranger's tower is twice as wide (92 blocks), its floors a fifth taller and its throne hall twice as high; the Magma Knight's maze is twice as wide (220 blocks) with halls 1.6 times as tall and a throne room twice as wide and 1.4 times as tall. The realms are not tall enough for the castles to double in height as well. In old worlds the castles are torn down and rebuilt at the new size where they stand, and their lords and guards return; a maze with no room to grow stays as it was.
  • Smoke in unseen rooms: the rooms of both castles you have not entered are hidden by thick, drifting smoke, white in the tower and red in the maze, like battle smoke but denser; each room clears when you step into it.

Beta 0.9.103

  • Lava through plants: lava flows into (and burns) everything water flows through or washes away: plants, saplings, glow plants, grove and magma plants, cobwebs and rugs. The list of what lava could enter was made when the game first loaded its blocks, so everything added since then stopped lava like a wall. Lava still stops at chests, doors, hives and furniture, hardens on water, burns wood nearby and hurts you as before.
  • The antler rod: the shaft is antler, white from end to end, with no tines.
  • The bug net: the rim sits across the end of the shaft facing the way you swing, and the mesh runs forward to a tip at the top.

Beta 0.9.102

  • Crystal golems in the colours of their caves: jade green in jade caves, rose pink in rose quartz caves, azure blue, white quartz in dripstone caves, amber in root caves, ivory bone crystal in fossil caves, moss green-gold in the overgrown caverns, and sapphire, emerald or ruby in the gem caves (plain caves keep the sapphire blue). The colour is picked where the golem first appears; friends see the same golem in the same colour. Health, damage and drops are unchanged.

Beta 0.9.101

  • The net catches what the swing reaches: every insect in the swing's path (3 blocks ahead, a little behind, 2.5 blocks up and down) or within 2.2 blocks of where you aim goes in the net, and each insect counts as a body about half a block wide, not a point, so a near miss still catches. Before, only insects within about a block of the exact aim point were taken.
  • A truer net: the handle meets the rim at the net's widest part and the mesh hangs down to a point.
  • Sounds: a soft chirp when an insect is caught, a tiny pop when one dies.

Beta 0.9.100

  • Insect bait lasts and lures: with an insect on the hook the catch is rolled twice and you get the better one; with a sky or magma insect it is rolled three times. After a catch the insect is kept 35% of the time (70% on the antler rod). Worms and grubs are used up as before.
  • Insects of the realms: cloud moths, sky skimmers and sun beetles in the sky realm; ember moths, cinder beetles and ash wasps in the magma realm. As bait they count as a rod two tiers better and bring treasure about one cast in five.
  • Insects of the caves: jade weevils, rose moths, azure midges (azure caves), drip mites (dripstone caves), root crickets (root caves), bone beetles (fossil caves) and lantern flies (overgrown caverns). Plain caves keep beetles and glow flies.
  • More animals: up to 9 animals near you instead of 6, and the next ones come after 3.5 seconds instead of 5.
  • Bucks and antlers: about half of the deer are bucks with big antlers; does have none. A buck kept in a pen sheds antlers into the trough chest on 40% of mornings, and a hunted buck drops them half the time. Two antlers, an iron bar and four planks make the antler rod at a workbench: as good as a gold rod, and insects on its hook bite 25% sooner and are kept 70% of the time.
  • The fishing cast at full strength: a full charge now lands on the farthest water within 11.5 blocks instead of refusing when the water was not at that exact distance, and the bobber stays on the line (a 13-14 block cast was dropped at once before). A quick tap still casts 5 blocks out.

Beta 0.9.99

  • Bee hives: hives hang under the leaves of trees near flowers (about one for every 45 columns of woodland, in old worlds too where you have not been). Build your own at a workbench from 8 planks and a jar of honey and hang it under any block. Bees born near a hive belong to it and fly between the flowers and the hive.
  • Honey: a hive with at least two flowers within 12 blocks fills each morning and drips when full; tap it within reach to take two jars of honey (food, and the Herbalist, Quartermaster and forester ask for it). A full hive cannot be mined by a tap, only emptied; an empty one can be picked up again.
  • Colours: butterflies come in ten colours and beetles in eight; the caught item is the same.
  • The bug net swings like a pickaxe: the same arm-and-tool swing as mining, one swing after another while you hold the button, with a catch at the start of every swing.

Beta 0.9.98

  • Insects die in one hit: swing a sword, pickaxe, axe, hoe, hammer or fishing rod and every insect in the swing's path dies at once: about 3 blocks ahead of you, a little behind, 2.5 blocks up and down, and anything within 2 blocks of where you aim. Before, only an insect right at the aim point was hit, so a bug flying past the swing often lived. The bug net is the only thing that takes them alive.

Beta 0.9.97

  • Insects: eight kinds live around you and come and go as you move: butterflies and bees by flowers (by day, not in rain), dragonflies over water (by day), fireflies over grass (at night, glowing), crickets in tall grass and sand fleas on beaches (they hop away when you come close), beetles in the woods and in caves, and glow flies by glowing mushrooms underground. They have their own Insects section in this wiki.
  • The bug net: 3 sticks and 2 wool at a workbench, or 12 coins at the Fisher. Swing it at an insect within reach and it goes in your bag alive. Swing any other tool or weapon at an insect, or hit it with an arrow, bolt or spell, and it dies and leaves nothing.
  • Insects are bait: butterflies, bees, dragonflies, fireflies and beetles join crickets, sand fleas and glow flies as fishing bait (each cast uses the first bait in your bag), and all of them sell to traders. Crickets, sand fleas and glow flies no longer drop as items from tall grass, sand or glowing mushrooms: those now startle a live one out for you to net. Worms and grubs are still dug up and found in logs. The Fisher no longer sells crickets; he sells the net instead.
  • Shared worlds: each player sees and catches their own insects.

Beta 0.9.96

  • Two taps to place a building: the first tap or click shows the box where the building would go, green when it fits and red when something is in the way, with a message saying which. A second tap inside a green box builds it there (the spot is checked again first); a tap anywhere else moves the box, and a tap on a red box just looks again. On a computer the box still follows the mouse until the first click.
  • Building spots back to the older rule: a building clears trees, grass and plants as before, plus mist clouds, bushes, ivy, hanging moss and roots (mist hanging at tower height was what refused towers on clear, flat ground). The 0.9.93 rule that let a building take any loose block and dug up to two rows of ground away was undone: it let buildings sink into bumps of ground.
  • The animal pen is a barn: plank walls with a door at each end, a stepped roof, a loft over the left end, a trough chest and a torch inside (40 planks, 2 doors, a chest, a torch). Penned animals live inside it as before. Pens built earlier stay as they are.

Beta 0.9.95

  • Falling buildings crumble down: every building that loses its base now drops straight down where it stood (towers no longer tip over like a tree). Its blocks work loose and drift apart as it falls, the upper ones lagging behind, with dust trailing off, and it lands as a heap of crumbled back walls as before.

Beta 0.9.94

  • War machines hold their ground: a crewed machine plants itself as soon as an enemy is in range (or you mark a target for it) and fires from there; it waits 4 seconds after its last target before following you again, stops well short of you, and only starts again once you are clearly farther off. Longer range: ballista 34 blocks (was 24), cannon 28 (was 18), trebuchet 50 (was 36). It no longer shuffles back and forth or hops in place.
  • Enemy formations: enemy soldiers in a fight are grouped by who stands within 10 blocks of each other. A group of three or more with at least two kinds of soldier lines up facing you: the toughest melee in the front rank (three to a rank), the other melee in ranks behind it holding their place, and archers and mages about three blocks behind the last melee rank, shooting from there. Front-rank enemies facing you brace like yours (less damage and knockback). Groups of one kind, posted guards, warlords and bosses fight as before.

Beta 0.9.93

  • Building spots: a building may replace mist, bushes, flowers, vines and other loose natural bits as well as grass, plants and trees, and up to two rows of natural ground at the bottom of its spot are dug away. Mist hanging at about tower height used to refuse watchtowers and keeps on flat ground.
  • Cancel on phones: a small Cancel button at the bottom middle of the screen while you place a building (computers keep the larger one at the top).
  • Blasts and back walls: bombs, cannonballs and trebuchet boulders clear the back walls of the cells they blow open (a wall still standing in front keeps its back wall). The same protections apply as for blocks, and your Quartermaster puts back any your village loses.
  • Enemy war machines set up once the army is within 30 blocks of your village edge (it used to be 50 blocks from the middle, which a wide village never let them reach).
  • Test world tools (God menu, World tab): day -1, +1, +5 or any day you type; dawn, noon, dusk or midnight; claim the village at once; an enemy raid; a siege army with cannons and a trebuchet; bring the attackers to your village edge at once; a dragon raid on your village; enemy war machines in front of you; a monster wave; four boulders on your village to try the repairs.

Beta 0.9.92

  • Repairs: whatever blasts, fire, scorching and falling buildings change in your village is remembered block by block. Talk to your Quartermaster and choose Repair the village: his crew puts it back from the ground up over part of a day (about a tenth of a day for a small hole, most of a day for a lot), faster while you sleep. Repairs are free.
  • Capturing a base: when a faction is beaten, its last soldiers surrender and join your army at once (you are no longer asked). The faction's own Quartermaster, Recruiter and Beast trainer leave and a new Quartermaster arrives at its castle. Talk to him and choose Fix up the castle and claim it: what the battle broke (blasts, fire, falling buildings and what you dug out) is put back, the castle is yours, and a Recruiter and a Beast trainer move in. Later damage there can be repaired the same way. If any of the three is lost, a new one arrives.

Beta 0.9.91

  • Siege damage: enemy cannonballs and trebuchet boulders break the walls of your village, houses, shops and castle alike, and scorch the ground around them. A village house whose base is blown away falls like any other building. The castle never falls down whole, chests, portals and unbreakable blocks are never broken, and your own bombs and machines still never harm your village.

Beta 0.9.90

  • War machines miss: ballista 75% hit, cannon 60%, trebuchet 50% (each 15% better when you work the machine yourself). A hit lands within half a block of the mark; a miss lands 2 to 3.5 blocks off for a ballista, up to 4.5 for a cannon and 6 for a trebuchet. Yours and the enemy's alike.
  • Bigger blasts: cannon and trebuchet explosions are about a third bigger, with three or four smaller bursts around the main one, more sparks and debris, more smoke and a stronger shake.
  • Smoke: all battle smoke lasts 50% longer (half gone in about 17 seconds, gone in about 70), and explosions put out more of it.
  • Scorched ground: around a cannonball or boulder crater, grass and dirt turn to ash, stone to scorched rock and wood to charred planks, like a bomb blast.
  • Buildings come down: when bombs, cannonballs or boulders blow away everything holding a building to the ground, it falls. A tall building (a tower) tips over its bottom corner like a tree, away from the blast; a wide one (a house, a keep) drops onto what is left beneath it with a slight lean. Wall torches, lanterns and platforms go with it. Where it lands it becomes a heap of crumbled back walls up to 6 blocks high, and its old back walls above the heap are cleared; no blocks are dropped. Anyone underneath is hurt. Anything in a chest or cupboard inside spills out. Castles, your village, the dragon's lair, the wizard tower island, the sky and magma realms and trees never fall this way.

Beta 0.9.89

  • Siege armies: from day 15, every 5 days (days 15, 20, 25...), one enemy faction sends a siege army against your village: about one and a half times a normal raid plus 3 soldiers, with a cannon and a ballista, and from day 20 a trebuchet as well. When the army reaches your village its engines set up outside your walls (the trebuchet farthest back), each with an archer crew. Scouts warn you the evening before each siege, and from day 10 count down to the first one.
  • Ordinary raids: unchanged. From day 5, each morning there is a 1 in 5 chance that one enemy faction sends a raid (about one every 5 days, but it can be two days running or two weeks apart). Raids grow by 1 or 2 soldiers every 5 days. From day 15 an ordinary raid brings a ballista only. On a siege day the siege army comes instead of a raid.
  • Explosions: cannonballs and trebuchet boulders burst in a fireball with a flash, a shock ring, flying debris and smoke, and a blast near you shakes the screen. Friends in a shared world see them too. Enemy siege fire hurts anyone in your village but breaks none of its blocks.

Beta 0.9.88

  • War machines: trebuchet boulders deal 260 (were 150) and crash through leaves and branches into the ground under a tree; ballista 75, cannon 110. Shots land where they are aimed. With the right stick, how far you push it sets how far the shot goes, and a crosshair shows the spot. A Step off button sits at the top of the screen while you work one. With crewed machines, a Machines button under the move stick marks one target for all of them; hold it to let them pick their own. Machines stay well back from the enemy in a fight and follow you up to 70 blocks before catching up (was 35). Every boat carries at least one machine, and any machine can be packed into your bag (Army window or beside it).
  • Enemy ballistas: from day 15, an attack on your village can bring a ballista with an archer crew, set up outside your walls. Each morning from day 10 you are warned how many days are left. Kill the crew or wreck the ballista.
  • Fishing: the storm rod (mythril rod, 6 storm bars, 2 sticks) only bites in a storm. Then it is the best rod there is, and more than a third of its catches are storm fish nobody else can catch: thunderfin, storm ray, tempest marlin and volt jelly. Out at sea it sometimes brings up a Kraken lure, which calls the Kraken back.
  • Bait: each cast uses the first bait in your bag. Worms (dug from dirt and grass) make fish bite sooner. Grubs (from chopped logs) bring bigger fish. Crickets (from tall grass) make fish bite much sooner. Sand fleas (from beach sand) and glow flies (from glowing mushrooms) bring treasure and rare fish. The Fisher sells worms and crickets.
  • Team charms: war banner (+15% damage), swift banner (+10% speed), shield banner (15% less damage taken) and mending banner (2% health a second out of battle). Each lifts everyone on your side within 8 blocks: you, soldiers, pets and friends. Made at a workbench or bought from the Jeweler.
  • Ten crops: tomatoes, corn, strawberries, onions, cabbage and melons join wheat, carrots, pumpkins and potatoes. They grow wild on grassy islands (break a ripe one for its crop and seeds) and on tilled soil. Animals eat them, for taming and breeding. They cook into tomato soup, corn bread, grilled corn, strawberry tart, vegetable stew, stuffed cabbage and fruit salad.
  • Farmhouse: a new building with a small house (bed, chest, two doors) beside a fenced, watered field of 11 tilled plots, planted with seeds from your bag as it is built.
  • Animal pen: taller fences at both ends, a gate, a rail behind the animals, a trough and a light; the wiki shows it with animals in it.

Beta 0.9.87

  • Vorthax follows you: once you have stepped into its lair it is awake. Leave and it bursts out after you (flying straight at you when it is far behind), then fights you in the open with breath, fireballs, dives and landings near you. Its army rule counts the soldiers around you. It gives up and flies home when you are more than 320 blocks from the lair, in the sky or magma realm, dead, or out of its reach for about half a minute.
  • Armies for bosses: the wiki has a table of the army each boss needs, a suggested size and a good mix of soldiers, and each boss page says the same.
  • Tool icons: pickaxes keep their shape with every gap in the head filled; hammer heads sit square to the handle with the striking face to the right; axe heads are turned clockwise so the small back piece rests on the handle and the blade points straight out, its edge along the handle (every tier, so they all match).

Beta 0.9.86

  • The desert: one wild island (two in large worlds) is a desert: sand on top, natural sandstone below, sand cave walls down to the shale, cacti and no trees. Older worlds turn the wild island you have explored least into the desert, changing only what you have not seen.
  • Falling sand: sand with nothing under it drops straight down and lands on the first thing that holds it; anyone under it gets hurt. Sandstone, natural or brick, stays put. A Sand wall back wall is made from sand, and two sandstone make two sandstone bricks.
  • Desert creatures: dune scorpions (their sting slows you), jackals in packs of three and swooping vultures by day; mummies (their wrappings slow you) and sand-throwing sand wraiths at night and in the sand caves. They live only in the desert; its camps hold them too.
  • The magma realm: wide open, with a black void and drifting embers behind the floating rock, a dim red light so you can see, and far more plants: fire ferns, ember blooms, and new blaze lilies, sulfur puffs and flame grass that glow. New creatures: magma salamanders (fast, their bite burns), ash wraiths (fly and throw fire) and obsidian beetles (heavily armored, curl up and roll at you).
  • Wizards: staffs hit 30% harder in a wizard's hands and every bolt flies on through a second enemy, like an archer's arrows.
  • Building: a Cancel building button at the top of the screen while you choose the spot, for phones.
  • The fishing village never lands on an orc island, a goblin camp, a ruin, a camp or beside a tower (new worlds), and factions never build on its shore.
  • The Goblin Beastmaster always has his scarred bear: it was tidied away with far-off animals; now it stays, and comes back if it went missing without anyone killing it.
  • The wiki: a Weapons side by side table (tiers, damage and every special weapon by type) and a clear rock layers picture.

Beta 0.9.85

  • Rain: the sky and clouds turn grey and the day grows a little darker while it rains, more in a storm; it fades in and out with the weather.
  • Camps: camps on the surface hold orcs or goblins instead of zombies and skeletons. Snowy islands keep their frost archers, brutes and skeletons, and camps in caves keep their undead.
  • Formations: the orders Follow, Defend me, Hold here and Charge are replaced by formations. Your soldiers always follow you and fight what comes near; while you move they run with you, and once you stand still they step into their places, face the nearest enemy and hold. Formations: Knights first (knights, macebearers, beastmasters, archers, wizards), Maces first, Beasts first, Wizards close and Archers forward. You stand at the front, in the middle (between the melee and the ranged) or at the back. The Army window shows the line as a small picture; Q changes the formation.
  • Every side in order: enemy archers and wizards keep a couple of blocks behind their own front line instead of running ahead of it.
  • Enemy towers: a watchtower, outpost or keep you have cleared of guards is no longer taken by itself: stand close and you are asked whether to capture it. A captured tower keeps all of its posts.
  • Placing soldiers: each building in the Buildings window shows a small picture of itself with your soldiers on its posts. Place soldiers opens a larger picture: tap a soldier, then a glowing post (or the post first); tap a soldier on a post to bring them back; Fill empty posts puts archers and wizards up first. Walking into one of your towers or keeps with empty posts opens it for you, and a checkbox there turns that off.
  • New world: the world name box is the first thing on the New world screen.

Beta 0.9.84

Save as file in the Report a bug screen works on the claude.ai page too: the page asks you first, then saves the report as a text file.

Beta 0.9.83

Gold ore is a fifth rarer. The second floor of your castle is one open room: the small-room wall and its door are gone, also in older castles. Crafting follows you: a recipe shows only while its workbench or furnace is within reach, so walking away from the furnace drops the cooking recipes and reaching a workbench brings its recipes up, with a line that says where you are. Open doors have depth: the leaf stays behind you and the frame passes in front of you. Enemy factions send scouting parties into the caves only under an island they hold. Torches placed on slopes stand on the slope and lean with it. In chests the Sort order is a button in the game font (each click sorts, then offers the next order) and an X at the top closes the chest. A miner, woodcutter or farmer who gets stuck for a while is put back at the hut or lodge and hands in what it carries. A tree struck by lightning (or lit by any fire) keeps a charred-wood back wall behind its trunk and crown, so the burnt pieces no longer look like blocks floating in the air.

Goblin outposts and orc camps fill up again: a camp you emptied gets new residents after three days while nobody is near, unless you conquered its island. Snow islands are snow and ice all the way down to the grey shale layer, with snow cave walls, and a Snow wall back wall can be made from snow at a workbench. Fights are tidier: at most three fighters stand on one block, drawn far, middle and near so you can see all three; the rest spread to the blocks beside.

Every building you place stands on solid ground: pits, caves under a thin crust and slopes under the footprint are filled with stone down to real ground, and village houses get dirt and grass the same way. Shallow holes under the buildings of an older world are filled once; deeper openings stay, since they may be your own cellars.

Report a bug: the pause menu and the title screen have a Report a bug button. Write what happened, then Send to the developers opens the bug list on GitHub with the report filled in (the version, your world, where you were and the last errors the game caught; nothing from your saves), or copy the report or save it as a file and send it any other way.

Controllers: plug in or pair a PlayStation, Xbox or other controller and press any button. Left stick moves, right stick aims, R2 uses what you hold, L2 the second action, Cross jumps, L1 and R1 change the hotbar, Square talks or uses, Triangle opens the bag, Circle crafting, the touchpad the map, Options pauses, L3 sprints, R3 throws the hook. In menus a gold frame moves with the d-pad; Cross presses, Circle goes back.

Test world: the Army window adds soldiers of any class for free (one, five or ten at a time) and can capture any enemy base at once; its soldiers join you and the base is yours.

More ore near the top: there is half again as much coal and a quarter more iron and silver, and the extra veins sit in the stone layer between the surface and the shale. Older worlds get the same in rock you have not explored yet.

  • Cobwebs: walk into one and you crawl and sink through it slowly, then tear free after a moment; a tap tears a web at once, with anything in hand or nothing. Enemies and soldiers get caught too and tear free a little later.
  • Vines: a chain of vines hangs as one plant. Climb them like a ladder: up to climb, down to slide, let go to hang; no fall damage on a vine. Many more vines hang under cave ceilings and overhangs; in the overgrown caverns some of them glow.
  • The abandoned village: where a back wall still stands, the house's side walls stand with it, ragged at the top, so nothing floats; stone, brick and planks beside a gap are crumbled or broken, and the back walls are cracked and mossy in patches.
  • When the repairs are done: a small note tells you to come back to the castle and talk to the drifters to choose their jobs; the jobs picker no longer fills the screen on its own.
  • Weather: from inside a building you still see the rain or snow falling outside.
  • Computers: the crafting menu opens as a panel at the right side of the screen, the bag at the left, so the world stays in view; a click outside closes either.

Beta 0.9.82

  • Fixes from playtesting: digging no longer turns the screen bright; with friends, the host's block changes reach them in the order they happened. Save and quit to title ends the multiplayer game, so you can host the saved world again straight away and a friend who quits can start a world of their own. A friend alone at an enemy village meets the same closed ranks as the host: bring soldiers.
  • Shops: a double-click on a shop row buys that item once. A shop house upgraded under a hill or another building keeps its low roof and tells you; clear the space above it and the upper floor is built.
  • Seeds: they go into the soil right under your feet, or under a soldier beside you, the moment you click.
  • Fence torches: a torch placed on a fence sits on the post and is drawn in front of you.
  • Ruins: cobwebs come in six shapes: top corners, bottom corners, hanging from ceilings and on bare walls.
  • Trees: a sapling only grows into the room it has; under a low ceiling it waits.
  • Diamonds and ore: diamonds only in chests of the deepstone layer and below, one or two at a time, in half of those chests; a quarter more iron and silver in small pockets.
  • Computers: the bag opens as a panel under the hotbar; crafting has a category column, group headings that stay while you scroll, and a Top button.
  • Multiplayer menu: Copy bug report copies the connection details and recent events as text to paste into a message.

Beta 0.9.81

  • Playing together, the Terraria way: your own game runs the enemies near you, so they chase, turn and strike on your screen at once; the host corrects them only when the two games drift apart. An enemy that reaches you hurts you right away, and the number you see when you hit one is the number that counts.
  • Still with the host: bosses, the dragon, the Kraken, enemy commanders, ships and animals.
  • Less traffic: the host sends an enemy that has not changed only every second message.
  • Mining and attacks: the body stays still while you work; only the arm and the tool move. A swing pivots at the shoulder and a shot leaves the hand, not the middle of the body.

Beta 0.9.80

  • Kills at once in a friend's world: an enemy you finish off dies on the spot; the host confirms it behind the scenes. Bosses and animals still wait for the host.
  • Every joined world under Continue: each world you have joined is kept with its code, not only the last one. Play opens your copy; Delete forgets it.
  • The option box: on a computer it sits centred above a villager's words; on a phone it stays put and the words move up above it.

Beta 0.9.79

  • Working pose: the arm reaches along the swing of a pick, axe, hoe or hammer with the tool in the hand, and the body leans into each stroke; placing a block reaches toward the spot with the block in hand.
  • Repairs while you sleep: sleeping during the fix-up moves the repairs along by the hours slept; you wake to a village further along.
  • The Quartermaster alone: until the repairs are done he is the only villager. The Beast trainer, the Recruiter and any wanderer arrive when the work is finished.
  • The ruin: cobwebs in the corners and under the ceilings, crumbled stone and brick where the walls fell, broken planks and rubble on the floors. The repairs clear all of it.
  • Water: sloped and stepped blocks beside water show the water in their open part. A puddle of one or two eighths standing alone, or water spread into a thin film over flat ground, dries up. A chest under water keeps its lid under the water.

Beta 0.9.78

  • Water and every block: saplings and spores, cactus, sugar cane, the glowing cave plants, the giant grove plants, wall shards, banners, paintings and rugs are covered by water and come back when it drains. Crystal blocks keep water out. A bucket poured onto a plant covers it instead of breaking it. Torches, lanterns, campfires and lamps still drop when water reaches them; furniture, chests and stations stand in water.
  • Buried grass: a grass block with a solid block on top turns to plain dirt (at once when you place the block, within about a minute otherwise). Trees do not count: grass stays under a trunk.
  • Saplings: a sapling or spore only goes on an empty cell; it never replaces tall grass, a flower, a crop or anything else.

Beta 0.9.77
