Other rules worth knowing

The rules of the game by topic. Each line is how the game works in this version.

Jump to: Controls · Saving · Multiplayer · Village · Army · Combat · Gear and crafting · Farming · Fishing · Animals · Building · World · Enemies · Bosses · Test world.

Controls

  • Keyboard: A and D move, W or Space jump and swim up, S drops or dives, Shift sprints. Tab opens the bag, C crafting, R the Army window, M the map, Q changes formation, F talks, trades, tames, cooks and opens chests, E fires the grappling hook, T trades with a friend, H hides or shows items, L is aim lock, K is quick save and Esc the menu. G flies in creative (and as the Scaleborn) and B breathes fire as the Scaleborn. Hold W or Space in the air to fly with wings. Change any key in Settings > Computer.
  • Controller: on by default (Settings > Controller: on or off, standard or swapped buttons, vibration, aim distance 2 to 8 blocks, dead zone). Left stick moves, right stick aims, R2 uses what you hold, L2 is the second action (fire breath for the Scaleborn), the bottom button jumps and flies, L1 and R1 pick from the hotbar, the left button talks and opens the chest you aim at, the top button opens the bag, the right button crafting, Options pauses, Select opens the map, L3 sprints, R3 throws the hook, d-pad up flies and d-pad down opens the Controller menu (every top-bar action). Buildings: R2 pins, R2 again builds.
  • Phones: joysticks by default; "Stick and tap" puts the move stick on the left (Settings > Movement stick side moves it to the right for left-handed players) and a tap anywhere mines, builds or attacks there. You always sprint while moving (Settings can turn it off), and a double-tap places a torch.
  • Phones: the game fills the whole screen on the first tap, and the grappling hook has its own small aiming joystick just left of the right stick: drag to aim, let go to throw.
  • Phones, Stick and tap: mining always takes the nearest block toward your tap.
  • Phone torch: the Torch button lights your way and your tools keep working; double-tap a spot to place a torch. Settings > Torches changes what a tap does while the torch is on.
  • Settings > Torches: choose what a tap does with the Torch button on, whether double-tap places a torch, whether tools rest while the torch is on, and whether holding Shift lights your torch.
  • Moving items on phones: hold an item for a moment to drag it to another slot, or tap it and then tap the slot where it should go. This works in the bag and in chests; in a chest, tap an item twice to send it straight across.
  • Phones: tapping an item shows its name. Tap the name to see its picture and every stat. Settings > Screen > Tapping an item chooses Name only, Short, Everything or Off.
  • On phones the top bar keeps Bag (with Crafting as a tab), Army, Buildings, the God menu in the test world, Menu and ⋯ More (Full screen, Hide items, Quick save, Show the mini-map, Hide the buttons).
  • Computer: hold Shift while moving to sprint 20% faster; Shift + click places a torch; the Full screen button enlarges the game.
  • Hold Shift to hold up a lit torch from your bag before placing it (Shift + click places it).
  • Settings has separate music and sound effects volume sliders. You can walk (A, D, W or the arrows) while a menu is open, and bag, shop, chest, army, recruit and map screens close once you walk 10 blocks away. You can attack while sprinting with Shift; Alt + click places a torch.
  • Mouse aim lock is on by default; L or Settings turns it off.
  • Scaleborn on a computer: hold B to breathe fire where the mouse points. The flames stop at the ground and walls.
  • Hold Ctrl and scroll, or pinch, to zoom the view. Zoom all the way out to open the map.
  • Near / Exact button beside the hotbar: Near takes the nearest block in the direction you aim, Exact the block under the cursor; it applies to the mouse too. Hide, under it, hides your army while you build.
  • Menus: the 1 to 9 keys pick a hotbar slot inside any menu unless you are typing in a text box; tapping another menu's button switches straight to it.
  • Menu windows (computers): drag by the top bar, resize by the bottom-right corner, places and sizes remembered; Settings > Computer sets where the bag and crafting open (left, center, right, or crafting with the bag) and has Put every window back.
  • Menus never freeze the world: the bag, crafting, buildings, army, map, chests, god menu and talking to villagers all keep the game running (only the pause menu pauses, in single player). Your hunger is full again after you respawn.
  • Every menu has an X in its top right corner that stays visible however far you scroll.
  • Buildings have their own window: the house icon in the top bar, or Buildings and defense posts in the Army menu. Crafting has a Show row to list all recipes, only what you can craft now, or recipes by hand, at the workbench, furnace, blast furnace or workshop.
  • Crafting is a tab in the bag (the Bag button, then Crafting, or C on a keyboard). Hover over an item in your bag to see what it is. Music turns tense when enemies fight you.
  • Music keeps playing under menus; pausing plays a closing jingle and stops it. Typing in the crafting search box never triggers game keys; press Enter to leave it. Seeds plant on tilled soil with a normal click, and you can hold to plant a row.
  • Screen: the buttons are icons - backpack (Bag), hammer (Craft), swords (Army), eye (Hide items), gear (Menu) and full screen. A live mini-map in the corner shows the area around you and other players; click it to open the map. The small arrow tab minimizes the icons and mini-map, and brings them back.
  • The eye (under ⋯ More on phones, or H) lists what shows on screen, all checked to start: uncheck the hotbar, bars, info, mini-map or a button to hide it at once.
  • Hover over any button for a label saying what it does; the god menu and fly buttons have their own icons.
  • Hide items hides the Bag, Craft, Army and Map buttons; your hotbar stays. Save is in the Menu (or press K on a keyboard).
  • Mini-map: the + and - in its corner make the mini-map bigger or smaller (4 sizes). Pressing - at the smallest size hides it, and a map button appears in the icon bar to bring it back. The M key always opens the full map.
  • Screen layout: in Settings > Screen the hotbar and the health, mana and hunger bars can each run across or up and down, sit in either top corner, change their order and be resized, with a live preview.
  • Checklists: the Checklist button beside a building, the village's growth, a shop upgrade or a recipe pins what it needs to the screen (up to 3), with check marks as you collect each item. It glows Ready! when everything is in your bag: tap it there to build, grow, upgrade or craft. Settings > Screen sets where it sits and its size.
  • Settings > Screen > Menu text size (Normal, Large, Larger) makes the words inside windows bigger.
  • On a computer the hotbar, bars, buttons and mini-map grow with the window until you move a size slider in Settings > Screen.

Saving

  • Saving: the game saves by itself every 30 seconds, and when you choose Save and quit or press K. Worlds are kept on this device by the browser or app you play in.
  • Save slots: choose World 1, 2 or 3 for each new world. On the claude.ai game page World 1 is also saved to your account; everywhere else worlds stay on the device only.
  • Copies of the game: the claude.ai game page, the play website and the computer and phone apps each keep their own saves. A world made in one does not show up in another.
  • World names: type a name on the New world screen (or leave it blank for World 1, World 2 and so on). Rename a saved world from Continue with the Rename button. The name shows in the save list, the pause menu and the multiplayer Recent list.
  • Desktop: install the game from pumped60.github.io/dragon-amulet-play in Chrome or Edge, or download the Mac and Windows apps from pumped60.github.io/dragon-amulet-play/download (phones and tablets: add the game to the Home Screen; the page has the steps and the install guide).
  • The wiki is its own app at pumped60.github.io/dragon-amulet-wiki. Add it to your Home Screen beside the game (iPhone: Share, then Add to Home Screen; Android: Install app). It works offline.
  • Steam: the Steam copy earns achievements (village, town, city, first tame, a conquered faction, each boss), saves to the Steam Cloud and is updated by Steam; the other copies are unchanged.
  • The scene editor, the tutorial and the test world keep their own heroes; they never change a saved character.
  • A new, empty character who enters a world that belongs to another of your characters starts with the starting kit.
  • A world that cannot be loaded returns you to the title screen and leaves the save as it was. A slot saved by another version of the game says so.
  • The demo keeps its own saves, apart from the full game.

Multiplayer

  • Hosting and joining: one player hosts a world and gets a room code; friends choose Multiplayer, then Join, and type the code. A game hosted on the claude.ai link and one hosted on the play website or the app cannot see each other, so everyone has to use the same one, and on claude.ai the very same link. Join needs someone hosting right then; to get back into a world you hosted, use Host a saved world.
  • Shared worlds online (on the game page in claude.ai): the host saves the world online every few minutes. Joining with the room code while nobody is hosting opens the newest copy and makes you the host. Your hero is always your own.
  • The Multiplayer menu lists your recent shared worlds with their codes: Host again reopens a world you hosted with the same code; Join rejoins a friend.
  • A friend's world you joined is kept on your device with its room code. Open it again from the Multiplayer screen, or host it yourself with Host a saved world.
  • Enemies and animals are spawned around every player, so everyone fights the same monsters. Hits on shared enemies count for everyone; bosses share one health bar. Names show above players, and a line under the dragon distance shows each other player's direction and distance. Everyone sees each other mining. If the host's world is creative, everyone plays in creative (including flying).
  • Playing together, each player's game runs the enemies near them (bosses, the dragon, animals and ships stay with the host); the host corrects drift and counts the damage a friend saw.
  • In a friend's world an enemy you finish off dies at once; bosses and animals wait for the host.
  • Enemies in shared worlds: the host runs every enemy, and they are just as active around each friend as around the host: night, cave, sea, forest, ice, sky and bird spawns follow every player, castle guards wake for whoever walks in, and enemy castles don't refill their guards while any player fights there.
  • In shared worlds every player sees the enemies and shots around them, wherever they are, and the group shares one limit on night and cave monsters.
  • In a shared world the host runs the dragon and the Kraken for everyone; they fight whichever player is nearest.
  • Multiplayer: everyone sees each other's attacks (the swing, its direction and the weapon held). Villagers are shared: when a guest hires a miner, the host's village gets him and every player sees the same one; whether the village is claimed and which castles are conquered are shared too. Players who join get the host's starting items (such as the recall mirror) and death rule.
  • Multiplayer: buildings put up by any player, soldiers stationed at posts, held defenders and faction changes are all shared, so every player sees the same world. Other players show on the mini-map (blue dots) and the full map (with their names), and when they are off-screen an arrow at the edge of your screen points to them with their name and distance in blocks.
  • Multiplayer: everything the enemy factions change in the world - new castle levels, watchtowers, outposts and their mine shafts is sent to every player, so everyone sees the same world. Players who join later catch up on these changes.
  • In a shared world everything matches: a tree cut by anyone falls for everyone, back walls and new village houses arrive whole, and a wanderer given a job by any player becomes the same villager for all. Only the host builds houses; a guest's choice is sent to the host.
  • Shared worlds: everyone sees trees fall and the logs pop out, friends who join see the host's explored map, and a player who leaves disappears with a message.
  • In a shared world every world change travels in order; a friend who falls behind the host's changes gets a fresh copy of the world.
  • In a shared world the host keeps every chest; a friend's change is applied by the host, so two players at one chest never lose or double items.
  • In a shared world, the host's world keeps running when the host switches to another app or tab.
  • Village growth, loyalty, tribute and expeditions are run by the host in multiplayer; any player can grow the village, and it grows for everyone.
  • Trading (multiplayer): stand within 5 blocks of a friend and press T (on a phone, the use button, then Trade with). Both of you put items and coins on the table and press Accept; changing an offer takes back both Accepts.
  • Boss loot in shared worlds: every player gets a copy. Coins and other ground items go to the first player who picks them up.
  • Multiplayer: every friend shows a gold-framed health bar with a heart over their head, green, then yellow, then flashing red when low. Soldiers show only a thin red line when hurt.
  • In shared worlds every player sees each other's armor, their soldiers' armor upgrades, and fires burning near them.
  • Boats with friends: jump or swim onto a friend's boat to ride along on the deck. Jump off toward land to leave. Soldiers sitting in a boat show for every player.
  • Bombs in shared worlds: every player sees the others' lit bombs, the pieces a blast throws, and the rubble from anyone's bomb. Rubble crumbles away after three days.
  • In a shared world a friend can hunt, tame and feed the host's animals; an animal a friend tames becomes that friend's own.
  • A trade cannot be changed for a moment after Accept, and both sides always finish the same swap.

Village

  • The abandoned village is a two-story overgrown castle with three houses; the Quartermaster waits inside, and talking to him starts repairs that take about a day. Burned villages burned from one upper corner, with charred back walls and plants growing through the ash.
  • Sleeping during the village repairs moves them on by the hours slept. The Quartermaster is the only villager until they are done; then the wanderers arrive.
  • Villagers: a wanderer arrives on about 1 morning in 5, and never more than 5 days apart. A villager works again once their job item is within 15 blocks and keeps the job at any distance; several can share one item; workers wait for their head worker. Shop houses match the village's stage.
  • Homes: every villager needs a bed, a chest and their job item at home. A closed room you build (walls, floor, roof, back wall, a door) with those three is a home; villagers of that trade move in.
  • Castle kit: the castle and keeps put back a missing notice board, chest or other job item.
  • Home helpers: the Quartermaster upgrades soldiers, the Beast trainer upgrades tamed animals, and the Recruiter hires new soldiers. New shop villagers get a new house if every house is taken.
  • Shopkeepers ask a favor from their own trade before upgrading their shop, and the Quartermaster asks for supplies before the village grows. The Jeweler needs a Town and the Archmage beaten; the War engineer needs a City and an enemy castle taken.
  • Shops: talk to a shopkeeper to upgrade their shop (levels 1 to 3, coins and building materials). Level 2 needs a Town and level 3 a City; each level sells better goods.
  • Shops: every villager buys all the goods of their trade (the blacksmith ores, stones, bars, gems and metal gear; the herbalist flowers, seeds, crops and plants; the cook food; the fisher fish; the shipwright wood and wool; the beast trainer hides, bones and meat). Hold Buy or Sell to repeat it. You can give any of your own villagers a different job at any time.
  • Shop prices: anything that can be crafted costs half of what its materials are worth (a material is worth 5 times its sell value). Selling never pays more than half the shop price.
  • The blacksmith sells weapons and tools, with better metals at higher shop levels. Metal ones cost coins plus one bar of that metal (for example 1 gold bar for a gold pickaxe).
  • Blacksmith upgrades: hold a tool, weapon or rod and talk to your blacksmith (or one in a conquered village) to upgrade it to the next tier. It costs 60% of the next tier's bars plus 40 coins per current tier.
  • The herbalist only sells seeds and plants you have found, and miners can only be sent for ores you have found (coal and stone always).
  • Fishers can be assigned as a villager job. On the map, choose whether each shopkeeper works at home or in a conquered village. The mine foreman lets you pick the ore for each miner.
  • The tailor: give a wanderer the Tailor job (one in a village). The tailor changes how your hero looks (skin, hair, eyes, face and clothing colors) for 25 coins and sells dyes.
  • Woodcutters, farmers and miners: the first wanderer given the job becomes its head and builds the woodcutter's lodge, the farmhouse or the mine. Then the Quartermaster hires more for 50 coins each: up to 5 of each, 7 at workplace level 2 and 9 at level 3.
  • The woodcutter's lodge, farmhouse and miners' hut level up to 3 after a favor for their head worker: more workers, a quarter more goods a level, and a loft.
  • Miners, woodcutters and farmers keep working and filling their chests however far away you are.
  • While you are away (up to 3 days) crops grow and your workers bring in their goods.
  • Miners: in the miner hut, choose which ore they dig. They fill a pack of 8 and pop back up to drop it in the hut chest.
  • Miners: each miner brings 1 to 3 items a day to the hut chest. Every boss or mini-boss you defeat (the Archmage, Magma Wizard, Goblin Beastmaster, Grukk, warlords, Sky Ranger, Magma Knight, the Kraken and the dragon) makes every miner bring 3 to 5 more per day, up to 30 each. Once a miner has brought his share for the day, he rests until morning.
  • Your miners dig real tunnels: from the bottom of the mine shaft they cut sideways, block by block, and carry what they dig up to the hut chest. When a tunnel gets long they start a new one a little deeper. The tunnels show on the map as they are dug - for every player in multiplayer too.
  • Miners: the mine shaft is kept open (platforms on the ledges, nothing solid between), a miner starting a trip sinks straight down it to the floor, and the rest of a miner's daily quota is put into the miners' chest at the end of the day.
  • Miners: a miner away 30 s comes back to the hut with his load.
  • Miners: an enemy hit never kills a miner; he runs straight back to the village, rests, then returns to the mine. Miners walk along their tunnels and climb the shaft smoothly.
  • Woodcutters: 6 logs each morning per woodcutter; they search the whole home island for trees.
  • Head farmer: choose per farmer crops, berries, insects, glowcaps, herbs, apples or honey; gatherers pick from real sources near the farmhouse when there are any, and the morning share is of that item.
  • Farmers: a harvest goes into the farmhouse chest as the crop that was cut plus seeds; empty furrows are sown from the seeds in the farmhouse chest, then a crop already in the field, then wheat; each morning every farmer puts 3 of the field's crops into the chest beside the grow meal.
  • Farmers: farmers cut the ripe crops in the farmhouse field and in every farm you built; each morning the ripe plants are brought in before the day's share.
  • Farmers gather only plants you have found yourself (the farmhouse field always). Woodcutters replant each tree they fell, and saplings grow into trees two mornings after planting.
  • Villagers hide in their castle while raiders are in the village, and come back out 15 seconds after the last raider leaves or falls.
  • Your Quartermaster repairs enemy damage to the village for free: talk to him after an attack.
  • Level villages: in your village and every faction village, the ground between neighboring buildings is flat at floor level (hills cut away, dips and cave mouths filled, trees removed). Outside the two end buildings there are 3 flat blocks, then a slope up or down to the natural ground. A new building levels only the gap beside it.
  • Village ground: raised ground is solid to the bottom; dug cells near home stay dug; dirt back wall under the base down to 10 rows.
  • Village paths: dirt at the first stage, the Dark slate path at the second, the iron walkway at the third, between every pair of buildings up to 40 blocks apart.
  • Villager jobs you change are permanent (saved with the village or keep) and, in multiplayer, every player sees the change. Items dropped in the host's world near other players appear for them too, and whoever picks one up takes it for everyone. Soldiers stationed at your posts never leave them, even when attacked.
  • Fountains: fill a fountain with a water bucket (a lava fountain with a lava bucket) and it runs. Every different kind running in your village, or within 30 blocks of anything you built, gives you and your soldiers its bonus: 2% for a small one, 5% medium, 7% large and 10% for a grand one. Each kind counts once, the same bonus adds up to 15% at most, and walking speed from charms, trophies and fountains together stops at 30% over what your boots and elixirs give.

Army

  • Your army can hold up to 20 soldiers. Teammates who fall far behind or get stuck reappear next to you once you are on land.
  • Soldiers move as fast as you and heal when out of combat. Fallen soldiers are gone for good, but a reserve soldier from your garrison takes their place and comes to you.
  • Army: soldiers who fall behind never catch up while you are up in a building; they leave enemies more than 22 blocks from you, and travel or coming back to life ends their old fights.
  • Realm portals take your party with you: every soldier who was near you comes along.
  • The Quartermaster in your castle sells permanent upgrades for all your soldiers: armor, weapons and training, 5 levels each.
  • Soldier looks: armor and weapon upgrade levels show metal tiers; stats unchanged.
  • Formations: your soldiers follow you and fight; when you stand still they line up three to a block by class (Knights first, Maces first, Beasts first, Wizards close or Archers forward), with you at the front, middle or back; the front rank and soldiers standing with two allies brace (20% less damage). In battles between the factions, cover beside a soldier stops 35% to 60% of shots.
  • Armies: you need your whole army (20 soldiers) to beat Vorthax; alone or with a small band he shrugs off your blows and heals. Enemy villages and outposts gang up on a lone attacker, so bring at least 5 soldiers; if you die there, the garrison heals.
  • Soldier posts: open the Buildings window, or press F inside a tower, keep or castle and choose Place soldiers here. A picture of the building shows every post; pick a soldier from your party for each one, or Auto fill all. Posted soldiers stay at their post, fight anything that comes near and are saved with the world.
  • Held posts: defenders in enemy watchtowers, outposts, the wizard tower, the magma fortress and on castle roofs stand at the window slits and battlements and keep shooting from there. They only leave their post once something hurts them. Soldiers you station at your own buildings snap straight to their post (beside a window slit or on the roof) and hold it the same way.
  • Posted soldiers: archers and wizards at posts in towers, keeps and on roofs shoot enemies coming at their side of the building, up to 22 blocks away and far below them.
  • Castle posts: a soldier post beside every arrow slit in the castle's corner towers (8), placed from F on a tower floor or Buildings.
  • Captured towers: clear every guard from an enemy watchtower, outpost or keep and stand close to be asked whether to capture it. Soldiers are placed on its posts from a picture of the building (Buildings window, or by walking in when posts are empty).
  • Your soldiers aboard your boat also shoot at enemy ships, not only at sea creatures.
  • Expeditions come back on foot to the Recruiter; talk to him to collect the loot.
  • War machines: a ballista, cannon or trebuchet is set up only with a free soldier in your party, who works it. It follows your army, plants itself and fires when an enemy is in range (ballista 34 blocks, cannon 28, trebuchet 50). Press F beside it to aim it at a spot (the crew fires at enemies within 6 blocks of your mark, or at the mark itself), to let it pick its own targets again, or to work it yourself. You field up to 5 machines and can pack one up again.
  • War machines hit: ballista 75%, cannon 60%, trebuchet 50% (15% more when you work it yourself). Blasts scorch the ground. A building whose base is blown away crumbles straight down; the castle never falls whole.
  • Enemy groups of 3 or more with two kinds of soldier fight in formation: melee ranks in front, archers and wizards behind.
  • War banners: the War, Swift, Shield and Mending banners stand 3 blocks tall on a pole. Use one to carry it, plant it where you stand or give it to a soldier; it lifts everyone on your side within 15 blocks. Nobody holds more than one. A carrier who dies loses the banner to the enemy who killed them, until that enemy falls.
  • The Mending banner heals everyone on your side within its reach by 2% of full health a second, out of battle.

Combat

  • Melee leaders: every beastmaster, knight and macebearer (you or a soldier) lifts everyone on their side within 8 blocks, themselves included: beastmasters +10% speed, knights 15% less damage taken, macebearers 15% more damage. One of each kind counts; enemy leaders do the same for their side.
  • Wizards: staffs hit 30% harder in a wizard's hands and bolts fly on through a second enemy.
  • Bows: arrows fly faster and straighter. Adamantite bow arrows home in on enemies. Fire bow arrows burn, blast bow arrows explode, and the tri-shot bow fires three at once.
  • Staffs: adamantite staff bolts home in. The Tempest staff's lightning chains to 2 more enemies, the ward staff gives you and nearby allies a shield for 12 seconds, and the ember staff sets enemies on fire.
  • Thrown weapons: the Adamantite sword and the Magma blade fly out and come back like a boomerang when you attack. The Adamantite mace is on a chain: tap to throw it, hold to spin it above your head, let go to throw it where you aim. The Adamantite and Kraken daggers are thrown one after another, up to 3 at once.
  • Fire weapons (magma staff, magma blade) keep burning enemies for 3 seconds.
  • Slowed enemies (frost runes, frost arrows) move at half speed for a moment.
  • Melee weapons reach 5% farther with each tier.
  • Mana refills by a share of your maximum mana every second: wizards 12%, everyone else 8%, but never less than 7 (wizard) or 4. Spirit gems and other mana upgrades make it refill faster too, and the mana ring adds 50% or 100% on top.
  • Food and healing potions share a 5 second cooldown.
  • Hunger (survival only): the hunger bar empties over about 5 to 6 minutes, twice as fast while sprinting. Eating fills it and heals you. Below 30 you stop healing on your own; at 0 you lose health until you eat.
  • Falls of more than 11 blocks hurt. Landing in water, wearing the feather charm or owning wings prevents it. A double jump, or a grappling hook that catches while you fall, breaks the fall: the damage counts from there.
  • Respawning: you, your party and your pets come back at your bed or home with full health and a full hunger bar.
  • Blasts from cannons, trebuchets and blast arrows break blocks (75% drop as items), except in your own and allied villages. Fire arrows burn grass to ash, char trees and wooden buildings.
  • Bombs: small and fire bombs are crafted at a workbench from black powder (2 coal and a bone); big and sticky bombs come from the Demolitionist, who arrives after the Goblin Beastmaster is beaten. Bombs break blocks; rubble crumbles away after 3 days. Your own bombs hurt you a little and never break your own village.
  • Battle smoke builds up from blasts, cannon fire and fires: it thins by half in about 17 seconds and is gone in about 70. Bombs make about twice as much as other blasts. Rain clears open-air smoke 3 times faster, storms 5 times and snow 2 times. Arrows and spells shot through smoke spread from 5% to 35%.

Gear and crafting

  • Tiers: every tool, weapon and armor line goes wood (or the starter), silver, iron, gold, diamond, storm, mythril, adamantite. Adamantite armor makes you immune to lava.
  • Tools: pickaxes break blocks and mushrooms only; axes chop trees, bamboo, cacti and mushroom trees; hoes till soil, clear plants and harvest crops; hammers break back walls and reshape blocks. Every tool has wood, silver, iron, gold, diamond, storm, mythril and adamantite tiers, and each tier is faster.
  • Silver gear sits between wood and iron in every weapon and tool line (sword, mace, daggers, bow, staff, pickaxe, axe, hoe, hammer, fishing rod), halfway between the two. It is made from silver bars, smelted from silver ore at a furnace.
  • Armor from weakest to strongest: wooden (3 defense), silver (4.5), iron (6), gold (12), diamond, storm, mythril and adamantite. Silver armor takes 15 silver bars at a workbench.
  • Armor pieces: helmet, leggings and boots for every armor tier; helmet and leggings 15%, boots 10% of that tier's chestplate defense (a full set +40%).
  • Pickaxes: the game does not say which pickaxe a block needs. If yours is too weak, the block simply will not break. The wiki's ore tables list what mines what.
  • Pickaxes also clear grass, flowers, plants and leaves.
  • Axes aim and work exactly like pickaxes, but only on trees, bamboo, cacti and mushroom trees (on phones the joystick targets the nearest tree part). Chopping any part of a tree brings the whole connected tree down, including pieces at ground level or under water.
  • XP, levels and the skill tree are switched off; boots, charms and other gear work as soon as you find them.
  • Bag: the hotbar plus 6 rows of 9; extra items stay on the ground. Drag and drop between slots, drag an item out of the window (or onto Drop) to drop it, hover for a picture and stats, and press F with several things in reach to choose. Every tool and weapon needs sticks, and any wood works in wooden recipes.
  • Trash and Drop: in your bag, pick up an item and tap the trash to throw it away. It stays in the trash slot, and a tap takes it back, until you trash something else. Tap Drop (or drag the item out of the bag) to put it on the ground.
  • Split stacks: select a stack of more than one item and use the Move slider (1, Half, All) to move only part of it; on a computer, right-click a stack for half.
  • Crafting: one picture per thing you can make. Craftable ones light up and come first; pinned ones (★) come before everything. Tap a picture to see what it needs, what you have, where it is made, and Craft, ×5 and Max.
  • Crafting: a furnace alone shows only furnace recipes, a workbench its own and by-hand ones, both together everything; the Craft panel stays in view while scrolling.
  • Crafting window: its title and color show where you are: Crafting, Workbench (brown wood), Furnace (gray stone), Workbench and Furnace (purple).
  • The crafting window has a search box and Craft, ×5 and Max buttons.
  • Crafting stations: most things are made at a workbench and bars are smelted at a furnace. Potions, elixirs and dyes are brewed at a Cauldron; weapons, armor and tools of gold, storm, mythril and adamantite are forged at an Anvil; rugs, beds, banners and other wool things are woven at a Loom; sweets are baked at a Brazier. Mythril and adamantite bars are smelted only at the orc blast furnace.
  • Crafting stations take space: the furnace and the orc blast furnace are 2 by 2, and the workbench and workshop are 2 blocks wide. Breaking any part breaks the whole station.
  • Nearby chests: opened chests within 15 blocks count as materials while crafting. The Stack button (under Near and Hide whenever such a chest is near) puts bag items into the chests that already hold them.
  • Chest window: your bag on the left, the chest on the right; Take all, Store bag, Stack (joins stacks of the same item) and Sort (by type, name, value or amount) along the top. Chests can be named.
  • A chest, shelf, barrel, cupboard or desk with anything in it cannot be broken; empty it first. A tap on a barrel, cupboard, desk or shelf opens it like a chest.
  • Buckets: craft a bucket from 3 iron bars at a workbench. Use it on water to fill it; use the water bucket to pour the water where you aim. Water poured on lava turns it to obsidian or stone.
  • Charcoal: burn a log of any wood in a furnace. Torches take a stick and a charcoal (or coal) and make four.
  • Grow meal grows a crop a stage or a sapling into a tree. The Breakdown bench, unlocked by beating both wizards, breaks crafted items back into all their materials. Things a shop sells for less than their materials are worth break down only if you found or made them, not if you bought them.
  • Special bows, staffs and thrown weapons are made from the Iron (Silver for staffs), Gold and Diamond weapon of their kind plus 4 gems: ruby burns, sapphire chills or splits, emerald heals, blasts or wards, amethyst pierces or flies farther.
  • Runes: each rune has levels I to V. Set more of the same rune into a weapon to level it up; level II needs 2 more runes, III 3, IV 4 and V 5. A different rune starts again at level I.
  • Ocean rune: three Ocean emeralds make one at a workbench. Set in a weapon, it lets that weapon hold a second rune and makes its runes work underwater. Without it, runes do nothing while you or the one you hit are in water. Ocean emeralds are very rare: a strong rod with good bait, or an Atlantean chest.
  • Armor runes: Thorn, Guard, Tide, Swift, Ember ward and Mend. Armor has two rune sockets, and an armor rune levels from I to V like a weapon rune. The runes stay when the armor is upgraded. Sold by the Jeweler, made at a workbench and found in chests.
  • Charms: use one to wear it. You can wear one of every kind at once and their bonuses add up; the bag's Charms worn row takes one off.
  • Grappling hooks: a permanent upgrade in three tiers (10, 16 and 24 blocks), each with its own rope and claw colors. Press E (or let go of the Hook dial on a phone) and the hook flies straight where you aim, bites the first wall, ceiling or ledge in line and pulls you there. You can mine, fight and use items while it holds you. Press jump to let go, or E again to fire a new hook. A hook falls when the block it holds is broken.
  • Wings: ten pairs, each flying longer and faster than the last. Moth wings (5 seconds, caves on the islands beside yours), Arcane (8, the Archmage), Sky (10, sky and magma chests), Cinder (15, the Magma Wizard), Tide (20, the Kraken), Cloud (25, the sky castle chest nearest the throne), Storm (30, the Sky Ranger), Ember (45, the magma keep chest nearest the throne), Phoenix (60, the Magma Knight) and Dragon wings (they never tire, Vorthax). Hold W or Space, or the Wings button on a phone, to fly; they recharge on the ground. Sky feathers are only worth coins.
  • Keychains: the Archmage, the Magma Wizard, the Sky Ranger, the Magma Knight, Grukk, the Kraken and Vorthax each drop their own keychain, and the Goblin Beastmaster drops a Stuffed Wimbly and Ograk's keychain. Clip one keychain to your belt (in the bag) for its ability, one at a time; it also fits in a trophy case. The only stuffed animals are Wimbly and Teddy, which sit on a bed or at your side for their bonus.
  • Fishing accessories: Bait pouch (Fisher, 300 coins; fish bite 20% sooner), Fisher's ring (workbench: 3 gold bars, 2 sea glass, a conch; 35% sooner and the better of two catches), Lucky lure (the Kraken; 50% sooner and one catch in five doubled).
  • Wards: Cooling band (Jeweler, 400 coins; no burning), Ward amulet (workbench: 3 gold bars, 2 sapphires, 2 rubies; no burning or frost slowing), Dragon scale ward (the Magma Knight; also no knockback and half lava damage).
  • Dashing: Fleetfoot sash (Jeweler, 600 coins; double-tap left or right to dash 6 blocks) and Shadow cloak (the Goblin Beastmaster; 9 blocks, untouchable while dashing). Swift boots (Jeweler, 350 coins) and Windstep boots (workbench: 4 gold bars, 4 wool, 2 emeralds) make you run 15% and 30% faster.
  • Wayfinder stone (workbench: 2 gold bars, a spirit gem, 2 emeralds): press M anywhere to open the travel map, and every trip is free.
  • Dragon compass: an accessory found in treasure chests (about 1 in 12, and always one in the goblin warcamp's gold chest). While you own it, the corner shows which way the dragon's lair lies and how far; without it you can't tell. The world map still marks the lair once you have explored it.
  • Astrolabe: a brass accessory found in treasure chests (about 1 in 10) or bought from the Jeweler for 220 coins. While you own it, the clock shows the season, the weather and the days until the next season; without it, only day or night and the day number. The test world starts with a compass and an astrolabe.
  • Sea treasures craft at a workbench: Merchant's charm (8 ancient coins, a gold bar), Dragon compass (old compass, 2 gold bars, a ruby), Astrolabe (old compass, a silver bar, a sapphire), Lucky lure (2 golden fish, 3 pearls, 2 gold bars), Shadow cloak (2 Kraken ink, a black pearl, 3 silver bars), Tidecrown charm (sunken crown, 2 pearls, a black pearl). 2 coral make 4 coral blocks.
  • The Scaleborn: a sixth class, always playable in the test world and unlocked for new games by defeating Vorthax in survival. He flies whenever he likes and breathes fire (hold B, or the Fire dial on phones), which uses mana. Vorthax drops the scaleforged weapon of your class; a new Scaleborn starts with every one unlocked.
  • Beds: a wooden bed is 10 planks and 2 herbs at a workbench, or 3 wool and 6 planks at a Loom; a straw bed is 8 wheat and 2 planks. Sleep at night to skip to morning. The last bed you used, anywhere, is your spawn point, and the recall mirror takes you there.
  • A thing never sells for more than it costs to make from shop goods. Sticks sell for 1 coin; worms sell for 1 coin to the Fisher only; dyes sell to the Tailor only.

Farming

  • Farming: till grass or dirt with a hoe, plant seeds and harvest ripe crops with the hoe. Ten crops grow: wheat, carrots, pumpkins, potatoes, tomatoes, corn, strawberries, onions, cabbage and melons. Every one of them grows wild somewhere in every world.
  • Farm crafting: the Farm tab in crafting holds every seed and plant (sapling, tall grass, fern, bush, red, yellow and blue flowers, berry bush, herb plant, sugar cane, cactus, glow mushroom), fences, the jack-o'-lantern and shears. All plant recipes are made by hand. Plants must be set on solid ground. Fences stacked on top of each other join into one tall post.
  • Growth dust (herbalist): sprinkle on a crop, sapling, bush or cane to grow it a stage at once and 50% faster afterward.
  • Bread: 3 wheat at a furnace makes 2 bread.
  • Sweets (at a Brazier): sugar cake (2 sugar, 2 wheat, an egg, milk), berry tart (3 berries, sugar, wheat), sugar cookies (sugar, wheat, an egg; makes 4), candied apple (an apple, sugar), golden fish feast (a golden fish, 2 herbs).
  • Saplings: every kind of tree drops and grows from its own sapling.
  • Saplings: oak, spruce, birch, palm, jungle, dark oak and pine saplings, and blue, green, red and orange mushroom spores. Pine sapling: a spruce sapling and 2 snow. Spores drop from 1 in 4 giant mushroom caps and grow on stone, moss, dirt or grass. The Herbalist sells them all.
  • Saplings: tree saplings are planted only on grass, dirt, farmland, sand or snow, on flat ground and in an empty cell; mushroom spores keep their cave soils.
  • Trees: a cut anywhere on the trunk fells the whole tree from the ground; a bee hive left hanging from nothing drops, with honey if full.
  • Trees: every trunk column stands on the ground; the block under a standing trunk cannot be dug out.
  • Falling trees: a chopped tree tips away from you until any part of it touches a solid block (the ground, a hill or a wall), then breaks into tumbling pieces of wood and leaves right there; nothing passes through the ground. Leaves are always drawn in front of the wood, while standing, falling and breaking apart.
  • Felling trees: only the leaves of the tree you cut come down; where crowns touch, each leaf belongs to its nearest trunk.
  • Trees: when a tree falls, its logs drop from the rubble where it lands; walk over them to pick them up.
  • Bare hands can knock down trees, about 4 times slower than a wooden axe.

Fishing

  • Casting: hold a rod, aim at water and hold to draw back the cast. A quick tap lands 5 blocks out; a full draw lands on the farthest water within 11.5 blocks. When the bobber dips and a ! appears, tap again quickly.
  • Insect bait: the catch is rolled twice and the better one taken (three times with a sky or magma insect); the insect is kept after a catch 35% of the time (70% on the antler rod). Sky realm: cloud moth, sky skimmer, sun beetle. Magma realm: ember moth, cinder beetle, ash wasp. Caves: jade weevil, rose moth, azure midge, drip mite, root cricket, bone beetle, lantern fly. Bucks (big antlers, about half of deer) shed antlers in a pen on 40% of mornings and drop them half the time when hunted; 2 antlers, an iron bar and 4 planks make the antler rod (gold tier, insects bite 25% sooner).
  • Storm rod: bites only in a storm; then it is the best rod there is and brings up storm fish and, rarely, a Kraken lure. Bait (worms, grubs and every insect) is used on each cast.
  • Fishing: a cast can bring up a painting, statue or other found-only piece: 0.4% per rod tier, never in place of an Ocean emerald or a Kraken lure.
  • Insects: butterflies and bees by flowers (day, not in rain), dragonflies over water (day), fireflies over grass (night), crickets in tall grass, beetles in woods and caves, sand fleas on beaches, glow flies by glowing mushrooms underground. Catch them with a bug net (3 sticks + 2 wool at a workbench, or the Fisher); any other tool, weapon or shot kills them and leaves nothing. Every insect is fishing bait and sells to traders. Each player has their own insects in a shared world.
  • The bug net takes every insect in the swing's path (3 blocks ahead, 2.5 up and down) or within 2.2 blocks of the aim, counting each insect's body; a caught insect chirps and a killed one pops.
  • Bee hives hang under tree leaves near flowers; build one from 8 planks and a jar of honey and hang it under any block. Bees fly between the flowers and their hive. A hive with two or more flowers within 12 blocks fills with honey each morning and drips when full; tap it within reach for two jars. Butterflies come in ten colors and beetles in eight. The bug net swings like a pickaxe while the button is held.

Animals

  • Animals: 50 kinds. Birds fly or wade; land animals live by island type (ice, desert, shore, grass) and time of day; dolphins, sea turtles, whales and manta rays swim the sea and never attack; octopuses, crabs, seals and walruses fight back once hit; skylarks and sky gliders live in the sky realm, fire toads and ember hares in the magma realm (safe in lava). Sea animals cannot be tamed. Up to 9 wild animals live near you at once.
  • Taming reach: with a taming whistle (or as a Beastmaster) you can tame an animal from about 3 blocks from its edge, even while it runs; flying birds can be tamed from about 4.5 blocks.
  • Taming: Beastmasters tame animals without food, cave creatures with 3 food and bees by touch; a pet bee stings once with poison, then dies. Other classes need the taming whistle and 1 to 3 food.
  • Tamed animals keep up with you like soldiers: they move at your speed when behind and reappear beside you if they get stuck or fall far behind.
  • Pets: Release and Slaughter for tamed animals in the Army window.
  • Only birds can be gatherers as pets: birds bring eggs, berries and seeds; hawks, eagles and owls bring meat. Gatherers find something every 20 seconds.
  • Breeding: hold an animal's favorite food and press F near it (wheat for cows and sheep, carrots or pumpkins for boars, carrots for rabbits, apples or berries for deer, berries or salmon for bears, meat for wolves, fish for penguins and polar bears, seeds for birds). Two fed animals of the same kind close together have a baby, which grows up in 2 minutes.
  • Baby animals: young animals have their own look (calf, lamb, striped piglet, chick, bunny, spotted fawn, bear and polar bear cubs, penguin chick, wolf pup) and grow into adults after a couple of minutes.
  • Fence pens: any straight line of 4 or more fences on the ground is a pen. Farm animals stay inside, cannot be hurt and produce goods each morning (see Wildlife). Fences are drawn in front of you.
  • Animal pens (Buildings): a barn with a door at each end, a loft and a trough chest (40 planks, 2 doors, a chest and a torch). Stand near your tamed farm animals and choose Put animals in; up to 8 live there. Each morning they fill the trough - cows give beef, sheep wool and mutton, boars and deer meat, rabbits carrots, birds seeds - and pairs of the same kind sometimes have young. Let them out any time.
  • Animals in pens produce or collect food each morning: cows milk, sheep wool, pigs mushrooms and carrots, boars grass and seeds, rabbits berries and carrots, deer apples, bears honey, polar bears and penguins fish, wolves bones, beavers logs.
  • Bird cage (workbench: 2 iron bars, 4 sticks): set a tamed bird's job to Cage near it to put it in (4 birds a cage). Caged birds lay an egg each every morning; use the cage to collect them or let a bird out. Birds never go in fenced pens. In a shared world the cages are the host's: everyone can use them and sees the same birds and eggs.
  • Pigs live in the grasslands by day and give pork; boars give boar meat. Beavers, hawks, eagles, owls and penguins each have their own meat, raw and cooked at a furnace.
  • Every animal of a herd appears on the soil of its own column, never inside a hill.
  • An animal caught inside blocks steps out to the nearest open spot.

Building

  • Placing a building: pick it in Buildings, then tap or click the ground. The first tap shows a box where it would go, green when it fits and red when something is in the way; tap inside the green box again to build it, or tap somewhere else to move the box. A building clears trees, grass, plants, mist clouds, bushes, ivy and hanging moss from its spot, but not rock, ground or anything built.
  • Buildings (the house button): a wooden hut (8 logs, 12 planks, a door, a torch; 1 soldier post), a Small tower (40 stone, a door, a torch; 4 posts), a stone watchtower (70 stone, 8 planks, a door, 3 torches; 6 posts), a stone keep (160 stone, 20 planks, a door, 6 torches; 6 posts), an animal pen (40 planks, 2 doors, a chest, a torch) and a farmhouse (40 planks, 2 fences, 2 doors, a chest, a bed, 2 torches). Place one within 40 blocks; trees in the way are cleared whole, and anyone standing there is moved to the nearest open space. The stone keep has a portal on its roof that joins your travel network, and its own Quartermaster, Recruiter and Beast trainer.
  • Small tower (Buildings): one room with a doorway on each side, arrow slits over the doors and a lookout roof; 4 posts; 40 stone, a door and a torch.
  • Watchtowers have arrow slits on every floor above the bottom room and on the roof, and the top room is 4 blocks tall. A watchtower has 6 soldier posts: two on each upper floor and two on the roof.
  • Towers: the Small tower and the watchtower have a three-wide hatch at the top.
  • Towers: towers, keeps and small towers carry a flag in the builder's team color with the builder's name; in shared worlds the ledges inside a tower reach every player.
  • Building stages: your houses, shop houses, woodcutter's lodge, farmhouse, miners' hut and every building you build or capture are wood in a Hamlet, dark slate in a Town and iron in a City. The castle is stone, then dark slate, then iron. Doors, furniture and arrow slits change with them. Wood and thatch burn; stone, dark slate and iron do not, and iron plate needs an iron pickaxe to break.
  • Roofs: hay at the wooden stage, faction-colored tiles at the dark slate stage, painted iron at the iron stage.
  • Shop houses at level 3: a red clay tile roof with the banner on top and a balcony with a hanging lantern.
  • The ground under every building (village houses, lodges, the castle, the mine hut and buildings you place) stays flat.
  • Doorways: when a villager you hire gets a new house, when the miner's hut is built, or when you place a building, the ground outside each door is reshaped for at least 5 blocks. Hills are cut away and dips filled, with a slope rising or falling one block per block to the natural ground, so you can walk straight out. Trees in the way are removed, and a ramp stops at the next building.
  • Buildings clear mist, bushes and small bumps from their spot. Bombs, cannonballs and boulders break back walls in the space they open.
  • Castle roofs are flat and clear for 5 blocks either side of the middle, where the portal appears; the parapet and roof guards are on the outer parts.
  • Guard posts can only be placed in a home with a bed, a chest and a job item; each is a soldier post in the Buildings window.
  • Arrow slits are solid to walk into, but arrows, bolts and spells fly through them. Enemies outside can shoot at whoever stands at a slit; the wall around it stops about half of those shots.
  • Trap doors lie in the floor: closed you stand on them, enemies never open them. Tap one, jump up under it or hold down on it to go through. Your castle's ledges from the great hall end in trap doors.
  • Only you and your allies can open doors in your village. Enemies can open doors in their own castles.
  • Doors: tap an open door to close it, even with water flowing through it; it holds the water back.
  • Doors: every door places with someone in the doorway (they step aside), under grass or aimed at its top half; aim at an old door to swap it; a placed door keeps its kind.
  • Placing blocks: animals step aside instead of blocking a block. Hold on an old block for 1.5 seconds with a different block in hand to swap them; the old one goes into your bag if your pickaxe could mine it.
  • Placing preview: the held block, wall, plant, furniture or door is drawn faintly where it will go.
  • Two-wide pieces: chests, workbenches and furnaces need flat support under both halves.
  • Floor pieces: chests, stations and furniture break when the block under them is mined; what they hold spills out.
  • Torches: can replace any plant; the plant (or its seed) goes to the bag.
  • Lanterns: a lantern must hang from something: a ceiling above it, a wall beside it, the back wall of a building, or a built block under it. Lanterns can't be placed out in the open. The village hangs its lanterns on the outside walls of its houses beside the doors.
  • Rooms with a roof and a back wall are lit by torches, lamps and lanterns; only windows and openings let daylight in.
  • Shaping: craft stairs, slopes and half blocks, or reshape placed blocks with the shaping hammer.
  • Furniture: chairs, stools, tables, desks, benches, barrels, cupboards and paintings are crafted at a workbench (planks, sticks, wool) and can be placed anywhere. They count as part of your base, so enemies do not spawn near them.
  • Furniture use: barrels, cupboards and desks hold 9 items; chairs, benches and stools are seats where you heal 3 times as fast (move to stand up). Each wood has its own plank wall and furniture pattern.
  • Furniture: tables, chairs, benches, stools, beds, bookshelves, cupboards, desks, barrels and workbenches come in oak, spruce, birch, palm, jungle and dark oak. Oak pieces take any planks; the others take their own wood's planks. Every plank sells for 1 coin and every log for 2.
  • Window wall: a back wall of glass panes (4 from 1 glass and 1 stick at a workbench). The sky shows through it and daylight comes in.
  • Rugs lie flat on the floor and come in six kinds. Crystals in the jade, rose and azure caves hang from ceilings and grow from walls.
  • Carpet: underground rooms carpeted wall to wall; a rug goes under a chest; the castle hall carpet goes under the furnace and the portal.
  • Log wall: a back wall item, 4 from each log.

World

  • Starting kits: Standard, + Recall mirror, + Grappling hook, + Mirror and hook, Extra supplies, or Bare hands.
  • Worlds: small, medium and large have a random number of islands (about 8-9, 10-12 and 13-15), and every size has every island type: the dragon's mountain, your home, two enemy factions, the goblin forest, the orc island, ice islands, and the Kraken's sea with a treasure island at the world's edge. The wizard tower stands alone on a small island of its own.
  • Every island keeps to its own creatures: goblins live only in the goblin forest, frost archers and brutes only on ice islands, orcs and ogres on the orc island, and zombies, skeletons and ogres elsewhere.
  • Orc island: rolling stone mountains to climb, and orc camps of every size - campfires (3-4 orcs), small huts (3-5 orcs, maybe an ogre), medium huts (5-7 orcs and an ogre) and big campsites (8-10 orcs and 1-2 ogres). Orcs come small with daggers, regular, big with warhammers, and with crossbows; ogres carry clubs. Stone towers at both ends have crossbow orcs on top and window slits.
  • The orc cave base: a shaft leads down from the tallest mountain into a great hall, two side chambers and a deep chamber, with 20 to 25 orcs and 5 or 6 ogres in all. The hall holds the orc blast furnace, the only place you can smelt mythril and adamantite bars. In the deepest chamber waits Grukk the Ogre King (13,000 health, two sledgehammers, twice an ogre's size) beside a treasure chest.
  • Goblin forest: goblin huts with 2-5 goblins (spears, daggers and bows), goblin archers in the lookout towers at both ends, and the Goblin Beastmaster's war camp guarded by 3-4 dagger goblins and 2-3 goblin archers. A captured villager waits to be rescued; once freed they head to your village to find work.
  • Ice islands are entirely snow, with small ice huts. Frost archers (their arrows slow you) and frost brutes (they shatter into ice) spawn around them and do not burn in sunlight.
  • Desert: one wild island is sand over sandstone with sand cave walls; sand falls when nothing holds it and hurts whoever is under it, sandstone stays. Desert creatures: dune scorpion, jackal, vulture, mummy and sand wraith, with scarabs at the pyramid camps.
  • Surface camps hold orcs or goblins; snowy islands keep their frost folk and cave camps their undead. Rain and storms gray the sky and dim the day a little.
  • Burned-village houses have open doorways you can walk through. Large caverns contain rock islands and pillars.
  • The fishing village never shares its shore with the goblins' wooden lookout tower.
  • Atlantis: each ocean wide and deep enough hides an Atlantean ruin on the sea floor: a domed hall of Atlantean brick, dry inside, with sea lamps and two gold chests of sunken treasure. Trident guards, sirens (their song slows you) and reef golems swim around it and come back each morning until the treasure is taken.
  • Oceans reach all the way down to the black stone layer, with sand at least 10 blocks deep on the sea floor, so the sea never floods caves. Backgrounds fade smoothly from one biome to the next.
  • Oceans are very deep, so digging under them is hard; sail instead.
  • Boats show how many they carry: a raft only you; a canoe you and 3 soldiers; a rowboat 4 soldiers; a longship 11 soldiers and a war machine; the warship, galleon and ironclad 20 soldiers each (no boat carries more than the 20 who can follow you), with 3, 4 and 5 war machines.
  • Boats are an upgrade: the best boat you own is always with you, and you sail it as soon as you walk into water (hold down to swim instead).
  • Frozen sea: sea creatures stay under the ice; projectiles still fly over it. Boats cannot be launched while the sea is frozen.
  • Sea ice: in winter, break the frozen sea with a pickaxe or axe to get a block of ice. The hole freezes over the next morning.
  • Factions: your faction is the abandoned castle you claim; the other two colors are enemies. Portals sit on castle roofs (and beside freed camps); click one to open the map and tap another purple portal to travel there with your army, for 15 to 50 coins.
  • Portals appear on castle roofs, beside freed camps, at the wizard tower and the magma fortress, at the two realm arrival points, at the Goblin Beastmaster's war camp once he is beaten and in the middle of the orc island once Grukk is beaten.
  • Travel: from the map you can travel with your army to the fishing village, your fishermen, and any enemy village you have conquered.
  • Fast travel: when you use a portal, the map has a Go home button that takes you straight to your home village for the usual fee.
  • A day lasts 10 minutes and a night 4. Monsters never spawn in lit areas or inside buildings.
  • Fog comes in three strengths.
  • Storms and rain last 12 hours of game time (the first half of the day).
  • Water falls through drop-through platforms, slides down slopes and stairs, and is drawn in front of furniture standing in it. Rain never leaves water on the ground: no puddles and no floods.
  • Snow layers melt outside winter, and you can build straight over snow.
  • The cold: on ice islands all year, and on every island in winter, grass plants, flowers, crops and saplings out in the open die and a snow layer takes their place. Snow builds up there and doesn't melt (on ice islands it never melts; elsewhere it melts after winter). Rain over an ice island falls as snow. Plants under a roof survive. In spring and summer, tall grass, ferns and flowers slowly sprout again on bare grass; crops must be replanted.
  • Weather can be switched off: choose Off when making a world, or Settings > Weather: Off.
  • Mist clouds float low over the islands, drift slowly with the wind for about 80 blocks, then fade out and drift in again from where they started. You can see the sky through them and you walk behind them.
  • Storm ore forms only where lightning strikes open dirt or grass in a storm, or around a lightning rod.
  • Water and plants: flowing water fills the inside of fences and covers grass, flowers, crops, bushes and mushrooms. The plants stay where they are under the water and are back once it dries; they do not grow while covered. Torches, lanterns, lamps and campfires that water reaches are knocked loose and drop as items.
  • Water covers saplings, spores, cactus, sugar cane, glowing cave plants, giant grove plants, wall shards, banners, paintings and rugs and leaves them in place; lights drop; furniture stands in water. A grass block with a solid block on top turns to dirt (not under a tree). A sapling only goes on an empty cell.
  • Water levels itself out and turns lava into obsidian.
  • A sloped block beside water shows the water in its open part. A lone puddle, or any thin strip of water on flat ground, dries up.
  • Water far from every player waits until someone comes near, which keeps shared worlds smooth.
  • Lava flows like water, filling in levels and spreading out until it is level, but slowly, creeping about a block every quarter second. You can swim in it slowly (hold jump to rise). It burns plants, wood, fences, furniture and leaves it touches, scorches grass to dirt, destroys items (not coins) that fall into it, and hardens to obsidian or stone where it meets water. Wading in lava slows you like honey and burns you unless your armor protects you.
  • Lava: jump while your head is out of the lava to climb out onto the shore. You can place blocks in and over lava to bridge it.
  • Lava sets you on fire unless you wear a ward accessory, and animals that walk into lava burn and die. Soldiers and monsters that touch it catch fire, except creatures of the magma realm.
  • Lava flows into and burns every plant, sapling, glow plant, cobweb and rug that water flows through or washes away; it still stops at chests, doors, hives and furniture, hardens on water and hurts as before.
  • Lava lies only underground: small puddles, pools and lakes, each with a pocket of air above it.
  • The bottom 3 to 5 rows of the world are unbreakable black bedrock.
  • Black stone: a 4-block layer between the normal underground and the magma realm. It cannot be broken from above, only from below (coming up from the magma realm).
  • Fire: wood, leaves, plants and grass burn with flames, spread a little and burn out to charred wood, ash or nothing; water and rain put fires out.
  • Grass slowly grows over bare dirt that touches grass and is open to the air (nothing on it, no water). A dirt block beside grass greens in about three minutes, and the grass keeps spreading. Tilled soil never grows over.
  • Slopes: natural slopes look like the normal block cut on a slant (a grass slope keeps its grass top along the slant).
  • Walking on slopes: players, soldiers and animals glide smoothly up and down slopes and stairs and stay on the ground going downhill, instead of hopping from step to step. One-block steps are still climbed by walking into them.
  • Caves are pitch black without a light: hold a torch, place torches, or wear a lantern. Ground below the surface gets darker quickly unless lit.
  • On the surface, a space is dark only when it is closed in on every side and has a back wall; a roof alone doesn't darken it.
  • Only trees in the forest cast shade when packed together.
  • The underground goes deep: the lowest caves above the magma realm are tunnels with lava pockets; cave floors are rounded and pools are natural bowls.
  • Underground rock comes in three layers: stone, then shale from about a third of the way down, then deepstone from a little over halfway to the magma realm. Gear is found only in cave chests at or below the shale layer. Chests are filled once and never refill.
  • Ores, from the surface down: coal just below the surface, silver from about a tenth of the way down to the magma realm, iron from about a seventh, gold from a quarter, emerald from 40%, diamond from 43% and ruby from about half way. Gem caverns (red-stone caves full of crystals) also hold gold, emerald, ruby and the only sapphire. Diamond ore needs a gold pickaxe or better.
  • Ore near the top: the rock between the surface and the shale layer holds less iron and more silver than the rock below. Where two ore veins meet, a block shows both ores and gives both.
  • Every underground biome holds the same share of coal, iron, silver, gold and gems as the plain caves; the gem caves keep their extra gems.
  • Ore veins take on the color of the rock they sit in. Every cave biome has its own glowing mushroom and plant. The overgrown caverns in the shale layer have giant plants, the toughest cave creatures (giant cave spiders, cave bears, thornback beetles, glow moths) and gold caches with better loot.
  • Cave walls vary between plain, cobbled, cracked and mossy rock. Every chest stands on two solid blocks; caves have no loose floating rock or sealed one-block pockets.
  • Behind every natural rock is that rock's own back wall, and each gem leaves a wall of its own color.
  • Most cave crawlways are two or three blocks high; one-block-high tunnels are rare.
  • Cave spikes: stalactites need rock above and stalagmites rock below; unsupported ones fall and drop as items.
  • Caves: big caverns hold rock pillars with ore, ledges with hanging stone and boulders; tunnels wind from narrow crawlways to wide galleries; small buildings sit beside the caves, each with a chest: miners' camps and stone shrines, and deeper down vaults and crystal grottos with gold chests and mushroom huts.
  • Magma realm soldiers have red skin and are immune to fire. Fire ferns and ember blooms grow there.
  • Magma realm: you arrive by a portal just outside the Magma Knight's keep. The realm is solid ground of scoria and basalt with obsidian pockets and glowing ember stone, and its own places: lava lakes, the magma forest, the gem caverns and the Burning fields. Adamantite veins grow in its rock once both realm bosses have fallen.
  • A world seed always makes the same world: the same land, caves, chests, villages and creatures.

Enemies

  • Difficulty: enemies grow stronger as the days pass and with every faction you conquer (shown on the Map screen). Faction soldiers are tougher still. Taking on a faction or an island alone is very hard; bring an army.
  • Monster limits near you at once: about 6 to 10 night monsters on the surface (by world size), 9 underground, 4 frost skeletons on ice islands, 3 ghosts per burned village plus a rare ghoul, 3 roaming sky soldiers and 22 to 30 sea creatures (they appear within about 15 blocks of you at sea or on the shore). Castles, camps and realm castles have their own fixed garrisons on top of that.
  • Enemies never spawn within 5 blocks of your buildings, crafting stations, chests, beds, doors or torches.
  • No enemy appears within 10 blocks of any player.
  • Zombies and skeletons burn in daylight. Wolves only attack if provoked, animals fight back against whoever hurt them, and no animals spawn inside your villages.
  • Goblins, zombies, skeletons and orcs only roam the top quarter of the underground. Deeper caves hold their own creatures: dripstone golems, root wyrms, jade mantises, rose serpents, bone hounds, shale crawlers, deep stalkers and moss trolls.
  • Flying monsters: bats, rose wisps, glow moths, ash and sand wraiths and vultures fly a few blocks over the ground.
  • Caves hold bats, jade crabs, rose wisps, azure spiders and fossil raptors, each in its own kind of cave.
  • Crystal golems take the color of their caves: jade, rose, azure, quartz (dripstone), amber (roots), bone (fossil), moss (overgrown caverns), and sapphire, emerald or ruby in the gem caves; plain caves keep sapphire blue. Looks only.
  • Enemy drops: no creature drops apples. Early enemies drop coins and one item; every other enemy at least two. Enemies drop building blocks only when made of them (golems, obsidian beetle and wyrmling, frost brute, charred treant, fossil raptor); crystal shards count as gems. Zombies drop Rotten flesh (bait; 2 make Grow meal).
  • Coins from monsters: a monster killed by an enemy faction pays its coins to that faction, unless you hit it at least once.
  • Enemies take fall damage like you do.
  • Enemies keep a little distance from each other instead of stacking on top of one another.
  • Enemies that cannot reach you head to the nearest door and wait there. Faction soldiers open their own village's doors (never yours), and new faction recruits appear inside the castle, beside their shipwright or just outside the walls. Animals never spawn on castle roofs or other buildings.
  • Hunting: enemy faction soldiers do not give up when you duck behind a wall. They work out a route (walking, climbing up to 2 blocks, and dropping down) to reach you, or the spot where they last saw you.
  • Enemies never teleport in view: a raider on a roof steps down to the nearest open ground, and a stuck war party jumps.
  • Living factions: each enemy faction runs its own economy. Its workers chop wood and mine stone and ore near the castle, the goods are sold to its merchants for gold, and the gold is spent on more workers, new soldiers (up to 42 at a fully grown castle), castle upgrades (levels 1-4), new watchtowers, outposts on free islands, and war parties. The Map screen shows each faction's castle level, soldiers, workers, towers, outposts and gold, plus their latest move.
  • Faction commanders: each enemy castle's warlord plays the game much like you. Near you, he walks out past his walls to dig down for stone and ore or cut down trees; far away, he keeps gathering. With enough ore and wood he forges better gear - wood, iron, gold, diamond, mythril, adamantite - gaining health, armor and damage and carrying a matching sword (iron in about half a day, gold in about three days, adamantite in about a week). Every day or two he sails to another island to explore and loot a chest, and he leads his faction's war parties himself.
  • Defeating a commander costs his faction half its gold and one gear tier; a new commander rises 2 days later, again and again, until you capture his castle. One enemy faction always lives to the west of your home island and the other to the east, never side by side. Scouting parties don't come for your island before day 3, and attacks on your village start on day 5 (see Raids on your village). Every attack is paid for and staffed by the faction itself: a raiding party (2-3 soldiers) costs 120 gold and 30 food, a war party (up to 10 soldiers, led by the commander) costs 360 gold and 40 food, and both are made of soldiers who leave the castle garrison - posted defenders stay, and at least 6 soldiers always remain at home. A faction short of gold, food or soldiers cannot attack, and a castle is weaker while its army is away. Survivors march home and rejoin the garrison. To claim a new island a faction must first defeat every enemy on it: it sends an expedition of 7 to 10 soldiers, and once the island is clear it conquers it by building an outpost near each end and a keep in the middle, all with garrisons (an expedition can also be beaten and driven off). You conquer a free island the same way: build a stone watchtower near each end and a stone keep in the middle; after that, enemy factions can no longer claim it. The Buildings window (Opposing villages) and the Map screen show each commander's gear and what he is doing.
  • Watching commanders: in the Buildings window (Opposing villages), press Watch beside a commander and the camera follows him wherever he goes; the bar at the top shows what he is doing and carrying. Press Stop watching or Esc to come back. Blocks a commander digs or chops go into his own bag, not onto the ground.
  • Buildings window: at the bottom, Opposing villages shows each enemy village's food, gold, workers and army strength (soldiers at home, in outposts and marching). Villages grow food from farms and workers; their soldiers eat it, and a village out of food cannot recruit.
  • Enemy castles hold about 15 soldiers led by a Warlord (450 health and up). He orders charges when you approach, sounds the retreat when badly hurt, and sends raiding parties.
  • Enemy villages have their own recruiter, quartermaster and beast trainer, who work for you once you conquer the village.
  • Enemy workers open the doors of their own village; builders only work on one project at a time per village.
  • Woodcutters plant a sapling where they fell a tree, so the forest around an enemy village slowly regrows near you.
  • Watchtowers have their door facing inland and window slits on every floor for archers and wizards. The goblin forest has a wooden lookout at each end, manned by goblins.
  • War: once a faction is strong, it sends war parties of 6-10 soldiers to retake a castle you conquered. Attacks on your village follow their own schedule from day 5, starting with 1 to 3 soldiers. If attackers hold a conquered castle for 30 seconds with none of your side there to defend it, the castle goes back to its old owners and you must win it again.
  • From day 3 there is a 1 in 5 chance each day of an enemy scouting party on the surface (Settings > World can turn raids off).
  • Raids on your village: none before day 5, then a 1 in 5 chance each morning that an enemy faction sends 1 to 3 soldiers, growing by 1 or 2 every 5 days, but never more than three quarters of your own soldiers plus 2. Raiders land on open ground outside your village, never on a roof.
  • A war party against your village lands from the sea at the east or west edge of your island; a message names the side when it sets out and when it lands, and a red arrow at the screen's edge points at the raiders while they are out of view.
  • Siege armies: the first comes on day 15 with a cannon and a ballista, with a warning the evening before. After that each siege follows the last one: held with ease, the next is 2 soldiers bigger and 5 days off; a real fight, the same size in 6 days; a costly one (you fell or lost half your soldiers), 2 smaller and 8 days off. A siege army is never more than 2 soldiers bigger than your own, and trebuchets come once sieges have grown twice. Enemy siege fire breaks the walls of your village and castle; a village house whose base is blown away falls (the castle never falls whole).
  • Surrender: once a faction's warlord is dead and no more than a quarter of the most people its island has held are left there, the faction surrenders at once and the soldiers still standing join your army. A new Quartermaster at its castle fixes it up and claims it, then a Recruiter and a Beast trainer move in.
  • Goblin island: only your conquest clears the goblin camps for good; sleeping does not empty camps or remove bosses; the Goblin Beastmaster returns 3 days after he falls.
  • The goblin forest sends at most 6 ambushing goblins a day (3 once the Goblin Beastmaster has fallen).
  • Enemy ships: a war party's ship has 260 (rowboat), 650 (longship) or 1,200 (warship) health, more as enemies grow stronger. Sunk crews drown after 45 seconds in the sea far from land.
  • Patrol ships hunt your boat and keep about 9 blocks away while their archers, wizards and cannons fire. They head home if you sail more than 90 blocks away.

Bosses

  • Bosses, in order: Goblin Beastmaster (bring 3 soldiers), Archmage (4), Magma Wizard (5), Sky Ranger (6), Magma Knight (8), Grukk (10), Kraken (12), Vorthax (20). With fewer, your blows barely hurt and the boss heals. The wizards fly and chase you; the Beastmaster vanishes and backstabs; a red mark means the Sky Ranger is about to shoot that fighter; when the Magma Knight glows, hit it with four fighters to crack its armor.
  • Battle plans: at Vorthax's lair, the Sky Ranger's hall and the Magma Knight's throne room you choose Ambush (soldiers placed on spots around the room; when the boss wakes, Vorthax is pinned to the floor or the knight or ranger stunned for 5 seconds), Waves (3, 5, 8 or 10 at a time, the rest wait outside and still count) or Charge.
  • Soldiers you hide in an ambush spot for a boss battle take 40% less damage while they stay at their spot.
  • Counting your army: a boss counts the soldiers and fighting pets you brought within 60 blocks when the fight starts (the first blow). The count never drops while the fight goes on, and the fight ends after 60 seconds without a blow on the boss.
  • Mini-bosses fight in set patterns: wizards fire fans, rings and homing orbs and blink away from you; the Sky Ranger shoots triple arrows, rains arrows from above and leaps away; the Magma Knight and goblin beastmaster dash, slam and sweep.
  • Allies: in the wizard tower the sky knights fight alongside the Archmage and his wizards, and in the magma fortress the magma knights fight alongside the imps and the Magma Wizard.
  • The Archmage tower is held by about 10 guards: 2 or 3 sky knights on the ground floor, 2 wizards on the middle floors, 1 wizard beside the Archmage, and a sky knight inside and outside each side guard tower.
  • Realm castles: the Sky Ranger's castle is a tall tower of five floors with the throne hall on top, climbed through gaps in each floor with ledges under them. The Magma Knight's keep is a wide maze of 18 rooms on three levels beside his throne room. Each boss hides behind the throne until you investigate it. Rooms you have not entered are hidden in thick fog, white in the sky tower and red in the magma keep. Stepping into a room uncovers it for good, and the guards inside only wake when their room is uncovered or when they are hit.
  • Boss castles: rooms in the sky castle and the magma keep stay dark, and their guards asleep, until someone steps into them; in a shared world a room any player enters wakes for everyone.
  • Vorthax follows you out of his lair once you have entered it, and fights in the open; he gives up beyond 320 blocks from the lair, in the sky or magma realm, or after about half a minute out of reach.
  • Wake or wound Vorthax and then leave his island, and once nobody is left on it his wounds close and he is back to full health.
  • The dragon's gold heap: walk into it to scoop up gold (5 to 9 coins each time, sometimes a gold bar); the heap slides down as you take from it.
  • The Kraken's treasure is buried on a small sand island at the edge of the world: look for the red X and dig down. It stays sealed until the Kraken is slain. The Kraken roams under the water and rises to hunt you, and the sea around it is full of sunken ships.
  • Ending: slaying Vorthax plays an epilogue and credits. Keep playing, or start New Game Plus with your character: a new world where enemies are 50% tougher for each New Game Plus.
  • The Dragon Amulet never fades, burns or is lost on death. It belongs to the hero who slew Vorthax in that world, and the world gives it back only to that hero. New game plus is in the pause menu once Vorthax is slain.

Test world

  • Test world: a world of its own with a God menu (give any item, upgrade any tool, weapon or armor, recruit soldiers, spawn any enemy or animal), super-speed flight (the Fly button, or G on a computer) and a world speed button in the God menu (1x, 2x, 5x, 10x).
  • In the test world, buildings, village growth and shop upgrades cost nothing.
  • Spectator mode (test world, God menu): enemies ignore you and cannot hurt you, so you can watch castles, factions and creatures act on their own. Free crafting (test world, God menu): every recipe, building and shop item is unlocked with no materials, stations or coins, and the All items tab appears in Craft.
  • Test world tools: the test world starts with a prepped farm a short walk from where you begin: 16 crop plots, a fenced pen with 2 cows, 2 sheep, a boar and a rabbit inside, and a farmhouse with a bed, workbench, furnace and a chest of seeds and food. The God menu's World tab builds another farm next to you, ripens nearby crops, sets the time of day, takes you to the test farm, forces the weather (natural, clear, rain, thunderstorm, snowstorm) and its strength (light, medium, strong), and starts a lightning strike, raids, sieges, dragon raids and monster waves. Forced weather lasts until you pick Natural. None of this exists in other worlds.
  • The scene editor (under Test world): a flat world of its own where nothing moves by itself. Place blocks, creatures, bosses and whole buildings as stickers, paint, select, copy, paste and undo, and take a picture of the scene.
  • The tutorial (on the title screen): a small world of its own that walks you through the first days step by step: mining, crafting, rebuilding the village, hiring miners and soldiers, farming, taming, building a tower and a night raid. A card shows each step, and you can skip a step or leave at any time.
  • The God switches are saved with the test world.
